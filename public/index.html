Taiwanese Poker

Split 7 cards into a 1-card, 2-card, and 4-card PLO hand. Two boards run. Everything gets compared.

Solo mode — no room needed. You get a random 7-card hand and try different ways to split it. Each split is scored against 150 independent board runouts, each against an opponent hand drawn from a pool pre-solved by iterative best-response simulation (see the strategy research in this project's README) — a strong, empirically near-optimal opponent, not a random one. Try a split, see its average EV, tweak it, and compare. Homerun scoring is always included.

Fast drills on the only real decision in this game, in three flavours: Arrange deals you a hand and you build the split yourself, graded against the true best of all 105 ways to split it. Estimate EV shows you a split and asks what it's worth, so you build a repeatable read rather than just recognizing shapes. Locked NLH fixes the 2-card hand and asks you to maximize the 1-card and PLO hands around it. Everything is scored live against the homerun-solved opponents Practice Mode uses. Misses are graded inaccuracy/mistake/blunder by the EV they cost and collected in a Mistakes tab you can re-drill.

How this game works
  • Each player gets 7 cards and splits them into a 1-card, 2-card, and 4-card hand (using all 7).
  • Two separate 5-card boards run out.
  • Each hand combines with each board to make the best 5-card poker hand: the 1-card and 2-card hands are "best of the pool" (hold'em style — even the board alone can play), the 4-card hand follows standard PLO rules (exactly 2 of your 4 cards + exactly 3 board cards).
  • Every player is compared head-to-head against every other player, per board, per hand type. Winning the 1-card comparison is worth 1 point, 2-card is worth 2, 4-card (PLO) is worth 3 — per board.
  • Exact ties push (no points change hands).
  • Homerun scoring is always part of the game: sweep all 6 comparisons against one opponent (both boards, all 3 hands, no ties) and the whole match total against them doubles, 12→24.
